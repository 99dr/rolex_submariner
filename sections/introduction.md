# 1. Introduction.

## 1.1 What Is the Rolex Submariner?

The Rolex Submariner is a mechanical wristwatch designed primarily
for underwater activities and developed as part of Rolex's Oyster
Perpetual Professional collection. Introduced in 1953, it was the
company's first wristwatch specifically designed for diving.
Its original design combined water resistance, mechanical timekeeping,
dial legibility, and a rotating bezel that allowed divers to monitor
elapsed immersion time.

The Submariner was developed during a period when recreational and
professional underwater exploration was expanding, creating demand
for reliable instruments capable of operating under increased
hydrostatic pressure. The original model was guaranteed waterproof
to a depth of 100 metres (330 feet). Subsequent generations
incorporated improvements to the case construction, sealing systems,
movement architecture, and display components, progressively
increasing the watch's technical capabilities.

A defining characteristic of the Submariner is its unidirectional
rotating bezel, graduated over 60 minutes. This mechanism allows
the wearer to measure elapsed time by aligning the bezel's reference
marker with the minute hand. The dial incorporates luminous hands
and hour markers to improve readability in low-light underwater
environments. Together, these features established the fundamental
functional architecture of the modern diving watch.

The Submariner is currently produced in two principal
configurations: the Submariner, without a date complication, and
the Submariner Date, which incorporates a date display. Both versions
feature an Oyster case, an automatic mechanical movement, and a
unidirectional rotating bezel. Current models are guaranteed
waterproof to a depth of 300 metres (1,000 feet).

Although originally conceived as a functional diving instrument,
the Submariner has progressively developed into a broader watchmaking
product. Its design, mechanical architecture, and historical
continuity have contributed to its recognition beyond the
professional diving environment. Today, it occupies an established
position within the luxury mechanical watch market and is frequently
referenced in discussions of the historical development of diving
watches.

From a technical perspective, the Rolex Submariner can be understood
as an integrated mechanical timekeeping instrument in which the case,
movement, dial, bezel, crystal, crown, bracelet, and sealing systems
work together to provide reliable operation under demanding
environmental conditions. Its development over more than seven
decades provides a useful case study in the evolution of mechanical
wristwatch engineering, materials, manufacturing processes, and
functional design.

## 1.3 Product Classification

The Rolex Submariner is classified as a luxury mechanical diving
wristwatch. It belongs to the professional watch category developed
for activities requiring reliable time measurement, enhanced water
resistance, and clear readability under demanding environmental
conditions.

From a horological and product-classification perspective, the
Submariner can be described according to the following
characteristics:

**Product Category:** Mechanical wristwatch.

**Functional Classification:** Professional diving watch.

**Movement Type:** Self-winding mechanical movement (automatic).

**Display Type:** Analog display with hour, minute, and central
seconds hands. Depending on the configuration, the watch may also
include a date display.

**Case Construction:** Oyster case architecture, designed to
provide water resistance through a sealed case structure and
screw-down components.

**Bezel Type:** Unidirectional rotating bezel with a graduated
60-minute scale for measuring elapsed time.

**Crystal Type:** Scratch-resistant sapphire crystal in modern
generations.

**Water-Resistance Classification:** Modern Submariner models
are guaranteed waterproof to 300 metres (1,000 feet), subject
to the manufacturer's specifications.

**Product Segment:** Luxury watch market.

**Manufacturing Category:** Swiss-made mechanical watch.

The Submariner is available in two principal functional
configurations:

- **Submariner:** The version without a date complication,
featuring a simplified dial layout.
- **Submariner Date:** The version incorporating a date display,
positioned at 3 o'clock and accompanied by a Cyclops magnifying
lens on the crystal.

Within the broader diving-watch category, the Submariner is
generally associated with professional-grade mechanical watches.
Its construction incorporates several systems specifically
intended to support underwater use, including a water-resistant
case, screw-down winding crown, unidirectional timing bezel, and
luminous dial elements.

However, its classification as a diving watch does not imply
that every Submariner is currently used for professional underwater
activities. The product also occupies a significant position in the
luxury mechanical watch segment, where design, materials,
manufacturing, brand history, and collectability contribute to its
market identity.

For classification purposes, it is therefore useful to distinguish
between its original functional purpose as an underwater timekeeping
instrument and its contemporary position as a luxury mechanical
wristwatch. These two dimensions coexist and are essential to
understanding the product's historical and commercial development.

## 1.4 Overview of the Submariner Collection

The Rolex Submariner collection comprises a family of mechanical
diving watches developed around a common design and functional
concept. Since its introduction in 1953, the collection has evolved
through successive generations, incorporating changes in case
dimensions, movement architecture, materials, bezel construction,
dial configurations, and bracelet systems.

Despite these developments, the collection has retained several
defining characteristics: an Oyster case, a self-winding mechanical
movement, a unidirectional rotating bezel, luminous display
elements, and a design intended to support underwater timekeeping.

The modern collection is organized primarily into two functional
configurations:

**Submariner**

The Submariner is the version without a date complication. Its dial
displays the hours, minutes, and seconds, maintaining a symmetrical
layout without a date window. This configuration preserves the
fundamental display arrangement associated with the original
Submariner concept.

**Submariner Date**

The Submariner Date incorporates a date complication positioned at
3 o'clock. The date is displayed through an aperture in the dial
and magnified by a Cyclops lens integrated into the sapphire crystal.
This configuration introduces an additional timekeeping function
while retaining the principal design and diving characteristics of
the collection.

In addition to these functional configurations, Submariner models
have been produced in different material and color combinations.
These include stainless steel, precious-metal constructions, and
two-tone configurations combining stainless steel with gold.
Depending on the production period and reference, variations have
also appeared in dial colors, bezel colors, bracelet construction,
and other design elements.

The collection has undergone substantial technical development over
more than seven decades. Early models featured relatively simple
mechanical movements and lower water-resistance ratings, while later
generations introduced more advanced automatic calibers, improved
case sealing systems, ceramic bezel inserts, and updated bracelet
and clasp mechanisms.

The historical collection includes numerous reference families,
each representing a particular generation or configuration. These
references provide a framework for identifying differences in
production periods, mechanical specifications, external dimensions,
materials, and design characteristics.

The current Submariner collection represents the latest stage of
this development. Although contemporary models incorporate modern
materials and mechanical technologies, their overall design
remains closely connected to the original diving-watch concept
established in the 1950s.

For historical and technical analysis, the Submariner collection
can therefore be examined through three complementary dimensions:

- **Functional configuration:** The distinction between Submariner
and Submariner Date models.
- **Material and design configuration:** Variations in metals, dial
colors, bezel colors, and bracelet characteristics.
- **Historical and technical generation:** The successive reference
families and the mechanical and architectural developments associated
with each period.

These dimensions provide the basis for the detailed classification,
chronological analysis, and technical comparisons presented in the
following chapters.

## 1.5 Terminology and Technical Conventions

This document uses standardized horological terminology and technical
conventions to ensure consistency, clarity, and comparability
throughout the description of the Rolex Submariner collection.

Technical terms are used according to their established meanings in
watchmaking, mechanical engineering, and timekeeping. Where
manufacturer-specific terminology is employed, it is identified and
distinguished from general technical terminology.

**Model Names and Reference Numbers**

The terms *Submariner* and *Submariner Date* refer to the two
principal functional configurations of the collection. Individual
watches are identified by their Rolex reference numbers whenever a
specific generation or configuration is discussed.

Reference numbers are presented in their commonly recognized
numerical format. Where necessary, additional identifiers are
included to distinguish variations in materials, dial configurations,
bezel characteristics, or production periods.

**Units of Measurement**

The International System of Units (SI) is used as the primary
measurement system throughout the document.

The following conventions apply:

- Length and dimensions: millimetres (mm).
- Mass: grams (g).
- Water resistance and depth ratings: metres (m).
- Pressure: bar, where applicable.
- Frequency: hertz (Hz) or vibrations per hour (vph).
- Time intervals: seconds (s), hours (h), and days (d).
- Power reserve: hours (h).

Alternative units commonly used in watchmaking publications, such as
inches, feet, and lines, may be included in parentheses when relevant
to historical documentation or manufacturer specifications.

**Dimensions and Measurements**

Case diameter, case thickness, lug-to-lug distance, and lug width are
treated as separate dimensional parameters.

Measurements are reported according to the following conventions:

- Case diameter refers to the external diameter of the watch case,
excluding the crown.
- Case thickness refers to the vertical dimension between the upper
crystal surface and the bottom of the caseback.
- Lug-to-lug distance refers to the maximum longitudinal distance
between the outer ends of the upper and lower lugs.
- Lug width refers to the distance between the internal surfaces of
the lugs where the bracelet or strap is attached.

When measurements are obtained from independent sources rather than
official manufacturer documentation, their origin and measurement
methodology are identified whenever available.

**Water Resistance**

Water resistance is expressed primarily in metres and, where
appropriate, in feet or bar.

Manufacturer-rated depth specifications are distinguished from
practical diving-depth recommendations. A stated water-resistance
rating represents a standardized pressure-resistance specification
and should not automatically be interpreted as a recommendation for
unrestricted use at that depth.

**Movement Terminology**

Movement-related descriptions follow conventional horological
terminology.

The term *caliber* identifies a specific movement design or model.
Movement frequency is expressed in hertz or vibrations per hour,
with the measurement convention stated where necessary.

Power reserve refers to the approximate operating time of a fully
wound movement before it stops in the absence of additional energy
input.

The terms *automatic*, *self-winding*, and *mechanical* are used
according to their established meanings. Automatic movements are
mechanical movements equipped with a winding mechanism that uses
the motion of the wearer to replenish the mainspring's stored energy.

**Chronological Conventions**

Historical dates are expressed using the Gregorian calendar.

Production periods are presented as approximate ranges when exact
manufacturing dates cannot be established from reliable
documentation. Introduction dates, production dates, catalog dates,
and discontinuation dates are treated as distinct chronological
events.

A model's introduction year should not automatically be interpreted
as the beginning of its full production period.

**Reference Identification**

Rolex reference numbers are used as the primary identifiers for
individual Submariner model families.

Where a reference includes multiple configurations, distinctions
are made according to case material, dial, bezel, bracelet, and
movement characteristics.

Serial numbers and production-date estimates are treated separately
from reference numbers. Production-year estimates based on
serial-number databases are identified as estimates rather than
definitive manufacturing records.

**Technical Specifications and Source Attribution**

Technical specifications are attributed to their respective sources
whenever possible.

The document distinguishes between:

- Official manufacturer specifications.
- Historical manufacturer documentation.
- Measurements reported by independent horological publications.
- Measurements obtained through independent physical examination.
- Estimates and values derived from secondary sources.

When different sources report conflicting values, the discrepancy is
identified rather than silently selecting one value.

Manufacturer terminology is retained when it refers to proprietary
materials, technologies, or systems. Examples include Oystersteel,
Rolesor, Cerachrom, Chromalight, Triplock, and Glidelock.

**Prices and Market Data**

All monetary values are associated with their respective currencies
and reference dates.

Historical and current retail prices are distinguished from
secondary-market prices. When prices are converted between
currencies, the exchange-rate date and conversion basis are
identified whenever available.

Market values are presented as indicative figures rather than
guaranteed transaction prices.

**Terminological Consistency**

The same technical term is used consistently throughout the document.
Alternative names and commonly used industry expressions may be
introduced in parentheses when necessary to facilitate identification
and cross-reference.

These conventions establish a common technical framework for
describing, comparing, and documenting the Rolex Submariner across
different historical periods and model configurations.
